# The claimed finite-time blow-up of the unforced three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations by Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, released on 20 November 2026 as a paper and a Lean development

On 20 November 2026, the same morning as the Riemann Hypothesis note, Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster posted a 79-page paper and a Lean 4 development in a personal GitHub repository, claiming that smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on three-dimensional space, and smooth periodic data on the torus, exist for which the Navier–Stokes equations without any force blow up in finite time, at every viscosity. In the Clay formulation this would refute alternatives (A) and (B) and establish (C) and (D) without the force that OpenAI's September claim used. The argument has two parts: a stability result, that one discretely self-similar solution of a certain kind forces blow-up, and the existence of such a solution near an approximate one that satisfies explicit inequalities, which the authors call a certificate. The certificate is not released; the paper gives its hash. That it satisfies the inequalities is checked by interval arithmetic outside the formalization. The authors say the write-up is unpolished and that they posted it rather than wait. Nobody independent has reported building the development or reading the argument. Everything here is open.
